Today marks the official beginning of development on The Columns.

We've been discussing the project for several months, but after a lengthy approval process, the company has finally agreed to move forward. The initial funding has been secured, the team is beginning to take shape, and we've started making some of the first decisions about what we're actually going to build.

It's difficult to explain the full scope of the project without making it sound ridiculous.

The simplest way I can describe it is that we're attempting to build an entire world. Not just a landscape or a collection of settlements, but something with its own geography, history, cultures, and inhabitants. Something that feels as though it has existed long before anyone ever thought to explore it.

We've decided to call this world The Columns.

At the center of everything will be a place we're calling the Main Hall. The current architectural plans describe an enormous marble structure, seemingly without an end, filled with rows of classical columns. We've spent a surprising amount of time debating the design, particularly the proportions and architectural styles. The general consensus is that the hall should feel ancient and familiar, almost like something that might have been built by a civilization whose history we've simply forgotten.

Beyond the Main Hall, we've begun planning what we're calling the outer worlds. Each will be a distinct land, with its own geography, climate, settlements, and people. Some will be relatively small, while others may eventually encompass entire kingdoms or continents.

The idea is that no two worlds should feel quite the same.

Mara Lee has been particularly involved in these early discussions. As one of our lead programmers, she's been working closely with the design team on how the different worlds will be brought together. She's also been pushing for something I think will become one of the most important parts of the project.

She wants the inhabitants to feel like actual people.

We've had several conversations about this already. Mara argues that creating a convincing civilization requires considerably more than designing buildings and giving people places to live. Every community needs traditions, relationships, conflicts, and a shared history. Individuals need personal ambitions and memories. Families should have stories that extend beyond the people currently living in them.

She made an interesting point during yesterday's meeting. A village shouldn't feel as though it exists because someone decided to put a village there. It should feel as though generations of people have lived there, built it, changed it, and passed it down to their children.

I think that's a good way of looking at it.

If we're going to accomplish anything worthwhile, we need to stop thinking about these places as collections of individual parts and start thinking about them as complete societies.

Of course, we're nowhere near that point yet.

Most of our current work is still preliminary. The design team is putting together geographical studies, the architecture group is experimenting with different building styles, and the technical team is working through the rather complicated problem of making everything possible through neural immersion.

There are still considerable questions about how much detail we can realistically achieve, particularly when it comes to the scale of the worlds we're planning. We've had a few promising early demonstrations, but nothing that comes close to what we've discussed in our meetings.

It will take years.

Still, everyone seems genuinely excited. The project has attracted a surprising amount of interest within the company, and we've already had several people volunteer to contribute outside their assigned departments.

I suspect we'll have disagreements about the direction of things before long. There are already several competing ideas about what the first inhabited regions should look like, and Mara has apparently started putting together an extensive proposal for the Main Hall.

For now, though, we're just getting started.

There's something rather extraordinary about the idea that we might be able to create somewhere that feels entirely separate from our own world. Somewhere with its own history, its own people, and its own stories waiting to be discovered.

I have no idea how much of our original vision we'll actually manage to accomplish.

But today, for the first time, it feels like we're going to find out.
